# Dated evidence refresh: regulation, noise, and cabin context

## Why this exists

Public high-speed aviation evidence is temporally unstable. This refresh keeps three categories separate:

1. current operative restrictions and authorizations;
2. prospective policy or rulemaking direction; and
3. active research intended to generate future evidence.

It also keeps general passenger-cabin requirements separate from high-speed practical proof.

## Reproducible result

`evidence_refresh_summary.py` reports four audited claims: one current regulatory constraint, one prospective policy direction, one active NASA community-noise research program, and one stable general cabin requirement. It reports **zero high-speed practical passes**.

The current FAA pages therefore do not support a simple “supersonic is now allowed” conclusion: the operative special-authorization/restriction context and the future policy direction are different evidence states. NASA Quesst remains a data-generation program, not a commercial noise-certification result. EASA CS-25 cabin rules provide a general baseline, not high-speed serviceability evidence.

## Limitations

- The audit records observed claims and temporal status; it does not perform legal advice or certify regulatory applicability.
- A web-page refresh is not a substitute for the controlling regulation, final rule, or formal certification basis.
- No source row closes the passenger, thermal, airport, economics, or high-speed noise gates.
